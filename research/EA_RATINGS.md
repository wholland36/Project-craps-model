# EA College Football 27 rating priors

The user-selected source is https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/ratings. The November 5 capture contains every player listed in the current Week 8 release: 10,939 unique players on 138 teams, across 110 pages. Store raw pages privately; distributed tables contain only player identities, team/position and rating attributes. EA player and team IDs are not ESPN IDs.

`research.ea_ratings` downloads public paginated EA data with an explicit rating iteration and verifies page counts, player identities and OVR consistency. An optional `--iteration 1-base` captures launch ratings. Exact duplicate source rows are deduplicated and reported; conflicting duplicates fail. A launch capture returned 11,730 rows and 11,727 unique IDs, so complete unique-player launch coverage remains unproven. Never assign missing players a zero rating.

For an explicitly identified player and his actual replacement, `rating_gap` calculates the signed OVR gap and a nonnegative snap-weighted talent-loss feature. A 95 OVR player replaced by an 80 OVR player creates a 15-rating-point gap at full exposure, not a 15-point change in the game's score. `scale_reports` requires explicit EA identity crosswalks and replacement IDs. It rejects future rating captures and does not infer depth-chart order, current injuries, or snap shares from overall ratings. The EA INJ attribute is a game durability rating, not evidence of current injury or availability.

A real points adjustment requires a position-specific coefficient estimated from observed, source-verified absence and replacement episodes. Expected workload lost must be measured relative to availability already embedded in the baseline. Quarterbacks use different roles from receivers or defenders, and offensive-line interactions require unit-level treatment. Actual replacement and snap exposure are not supplied by the EA roster. Uncalibrated rating loss remains a research feature; it does not become an injury probability or betting recommendation.

The 2026 Power 4 audit covers every completed schedule game with at least one ACC, Big Ten, Big 12 or SEC team. Its QB pilot uses explicit archived Game Day Out plus observed passing participation, not zero-stat inference. Launch ratings reduce contamination from in-season rating updates. Publication clocks remain unauthenticated, actual replacement attempts are postgame information, and a prior passing leader is a proxy for depth-chart role. The exploratory regression has only four nonzero-exposure paired games; its coefficient is not approved or activated. No non-QB coefficient is fitted without actual replacement and workload evidence.

## November 5 non-QB replacement rule

Wade authorized a verified same-team/position replacement where available; otherwise use the average of the next three strictly lower-rated teammates at that EA position. If only one or two exist, average those. Exclude teammates explicitly reported Out, preserve the rating vintage, and leave zero candidates unresolved. This is a replacement-rating proxy, not a depth chart or scoring-point effect. Stable ID ordering breaks ties without changing the averaged rating.

The all-season audit evaluates all absences jointly by team: position-level raw rating-gap sums, Out counts, extra absences within a position, and overlapping replacement pools. Multiple injuries must not be reduced to a single star-player deduction. Shared backups are flagged because independent sums do not establish feasible simultaneous personnel usage. Missing player identities, unresolved availability, snap roles, baseline-embedded absences and zero-backup cases block a complete team adjustment. No interaction coefficient or points conversion is activated.
